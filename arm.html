The Stack (in order)
Confirmed

Position-Specific Context

★ PITCHER

Full stack as written. The 5-layer system was built for pitchers. Long-toss program is non-negotiable on bullpen and game days.

★ CATCHER

Throwing harder per rep than anyone except pitchers — short windows, max effort. Lower-body warmup matters as much as arm care. Add hip mobility to pre-throwing.

★ 1B

Shortest throws of any IF position, but the most picks & stretches. Forearm and wrist series matters more here — emphasize J-Band #12. Plyo work can be light.

★ 2B

Short throws, but at awkward angles (pivots, flips, behind-back). Elevated rotation work (J-Bands 8–10) protects the pivot throw. Long toss is supplemental.

★ SS

Hardest infield throws — deep hole, on the run. Treat the arm like a pitcher's. Full long-toss program, full plyo, all 12 J-Band exercises every throw day.

★ 3B

Cross-diamond is the longest infield throw (~120 ft). Pitcher-equivalent durability for the shoulder. Long-toss out to 135 ft on practice days.

★ OF

Hardest one-off throws in the game (200+ ft to the cut). Long toss is the most important component. Pre-throw J-Bands non-negotiable since most OF throws are cold during the inning.

Daily-safe at scaled intensity. Bauer's full stack is built around one MLB start per week. For adult-rec / multi-position players, do the full stack daily at lower intensity. The risk isn't volume — it's skipping warmup. Arms get injured when cold, not when over-trained on bands.

Warm Up

Two tracked parts. Dynamic comes before you throw or lift — it raises tissue temperature and opens range. Static comes after, when the tissue is warm and lengthening is safe. Doing static work cold is the classic mistake.

Where this sits in the day
Order
  • 1 · Dynamic warm-up — below. Always, before anything physical.
  • 2 · Shoulder Tube — ~40s of cuff activation, immediately after the warm-up and before the bands.
  • 3 · J-Bands — the arm-care stack. Near-universal, even on a rest day.
  • 4 · PlyoCare — only if you are throwing, and never cold: the bands come first.
  • 5 · Long Toss — the throwing itself.
  • 6 · The rest of the work — drills, hitting, or the weight room.
  • 7 · Static stretching — below, after the work while you are still warm.
Rest day? Dynamic + J-Bands is still worth it. Skip the static block if you did nothing to recover from.

1 · Dynamic — before

Movement-based. Never hold a stretch here.

  • 01 · Leg Swings (Forward/Back + Side/Side)
    Hip Flexors · Hamstrings · Hip Mobility
    15 each leg, each direction

    Hold a wall/fence for support. Swing one leg forward and back like a pendulum, 15 swings. Then side-to-side across the body, 15 swings. Switch legs.

    The single best hip warmup. Do this every throw day.
  • 02 · Arm Circles
    Shoulder Mobility
    10 small + 10 large each direction

    Both arms out to sides. Small forward circles (×10), gradually growing to large circles (×10). Reverse direction. Both arms together — gets blood to both shoulders.

    Foundation movement, takes 30 seconds
  • 03 · Walking Lunge with Twist
    Hips · T-Spine · Quads
    10 yards each direction

    Forward lunge. In the bottom position, twist torso toward the front leg's side. Hold 1 second. Stand and step into next lunge with opposite leg. Walk 10 yards out, 10 back.

    Single most efficient dynamic stretch — hits hip flexors, T-spine, and balance simultaneously
  • 04 · High Knees + Butt Kicks
    Hip Flexors · Hamstrings · Heart Rate
    30 sec each

    30 seconds of high knees (knees up to waist), then 30 seconds of butt kicks (heels to glutes). Get the heart rate up.

    The cardio component of warmup — often skipped, always missed
  • 05 · Hip Circles (All-Fours)
    Hip Mobility · Active ROM
    10 each side, each direction

    On hands and knees. Lift one knee out to the side, then circle it forward, up, and back — as large a circle as your hip allows. 10 forward, 10 reverse. Switch sides.

    Specifically opens hip ROM — research links hip mobility to 20% reduced injury risk in throwers
  • 06 · Side-Lying T-Spine Rotation
    Thoracic Spine · Posterior Chain
    8 each side

    Lie on side. Top leg crossed over with knee at 90°, planted. Both arms extended forward together. Open the top arm out and across to the other side, following with the eyes. Hold 5 seconds. Return. 8 reps each side.

    T-spine rotation is the missing link in adult throwers. Don't skip.

4 · Static — after

Hold each. Only once the work is done and you are still warm.

  • 07 · Sleeper Stretch (★ PITCHER MVP)
    Shoulder Internal Rotation · GIRD Prevention
    2 × 30s each shoulder

    Lie on the throwing-arm side. Bend that elbow to 90°, upper arm in front of torso. Use the OPPOSITE hand to gently press the wrist toward the floor. Hold 30 seconds, 2 sets each side.

    Gentle. Pushing too hard strains the posterior capsule.

    Addresses GIRD (Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit) — the most common throwing-shoulder issue and the #1 stretch missing from typical arm care content
  • 08 · Couch Stretch
    Hip Flexors · Quads
    2 min each leg

    Kneel with the back foot up against a wall, couch back, or bench (top of foot on the surface). Front leg in 90° lunge. Drive hips forward, torso upright. 2-minute hold.

    Active version: flex into the stretch for 10–15 reps. Watch film while doing it — it's that long.

    For pitchers: focus on the lead leg's hip flexor. Most-tight muscle in throwers and hitters.
  • 09 · 90/90 Hip Stretch
    Hip Internal/External Rotation
    30s each side

    Sit on the floor. Front leg bent at 90° in front (external rotation). Back leg bent at 90° behind (internal rotation). Keep spine tall. Lean forward over the front leg to feel a stretch in the hip.

    Hip rotation translates directly to bat speed and throwing power
  • 10 · Shoulder Cross-Body Stretch
    Posterior Shoulder Capsule
    30s each arm

    Pull one arm across the chest, opposite hand holding above the elbow. Pull gently. Hold 30 seconds. Then move the same arm overhead, palm down, gently bend at the elbow.

    The posterior shoulder is the deceleration brake — tight here means injury risk
  • 11 · Wrist Flexor Stretch
    Forearm · UCL Region
    30s each arm

    Extend one arm forward, palm up. Use the opposite hand to gently pull back on the fingers. Then flip — palm down, pull fingers down. Hold 30 seconds each position.

    Reduces risk of elbow injury and bicep tendonitis — both common in adult throwers
  • 12 · Calf / Ankle Dorsiflexion
    Ankle Mobility · Achilles
    30s each leg

    Step forward into a small lunge with back leg straight, heel pressed to ground. Hold. Then bend back knee slightly to shift to soleus.

    Catchers especially — squat depth requires ankle dorsiflexion. Often missing in adult athletes.

5 · First Base — Split & Protect (optional · +bonus)

Position work for first base, built in pairs: every stretch is followed immediately by the strength move that protects it. Flexibility you cannot control under load is not an asset — it is an unguarded joint. The a step buys the range, the b step teaches you to hold it.

Already flexible? Then the b steps are the ones that matter for you, and the a steps are a two-minute check that nothing has tightened up. Not flexible yet? Run both halves and expect the a numbers to move in a few weeks.

  • 13a · Split Stretch at the Bag
    Adductors · Hamstring · The Range You Actually Use
    3 × 30s each side

    Front foot on a base or a step, back leg straight behind, hips square. Sink into the split the same way you would stretch for a throw in the dirt. Breathe out and let the groin lengthen. Switch feet — you stretch to BOTH sides at first base, not just your strong one.

    This is the only stretch on this page that is literally the position. Train the shape you get paid to hold.
  • 13b · Copenhagen Adduction
    Adductor STRENGTH at long length · protects 13a
    3 × 6 slow each side

    Side plank with the TOP leg resting on a bench or a partner. Lift the bottom leg up to meet it, lower for 3 slow counts. Start short-lever: top knee on the bench, not the ankle. Progress to the full straight-leg version only when 3 × 8 is easy.

    The strength half of the pair. Adductor strengthening on this protocol has the best evidence base of anything here — it was tested in football/soccer squads and reduced groin problems. Range you cannot control is range you cannot protect.
  • 14a · Knee-to-Wall Ankle Dorsiflexion
    Ankle · the joint the scoop is actually limited by
    2 × 10 each ankle

    Foot a few inches from a wall, drive the knee forward OVER the toes without the heel lifting. Back off until the heel stays down, then work that edge. Measure the gap so you can see it improve.

    A short-hop scoop is an ankle problem before it is a glove problem. If the ankle will not bend, the hips rise and the glove comes with them.
  • 14b · Heel-Elevated Split Squat
    Loading the bottom of that new ankle range
    2 × 8 each leg

    Back foot on a bench, front heel slightly raised, sink until the front thigh is past parallel and the knee travels well over the toe. Slow down. Bodyweight first, then hold a dumbbell.

    Pairs with 14a: you just bought depth, now put load in it so the joint keeps it.
  • 15a · 90/90 Hip Switch
    Hip Internal AND External Rotation
    10 slow switches

    Sit with one leg bent 90° in front and one bent 90° behind. Rotate the knees across to the other side without using your hands. Keep the chest tall. Slow — this is not a rep to rush.

    Picks come from every angle. So does the hip that has to get you there.
  • 15b · 90/90 Lift-Offs
    Turning passive range into range you own
    5 × 5s hold each side

    From the 90/90 position, lift the FRONT shin off the floor using only the hip and hold. Then lift the back shin. No hands. Five seconds of honest effort beats thirty seconds of leaning.

    This is the whole idea of the page in one drill: the range you can lift into is the range that shows up in a game. The rest is decoration.
  • 16a · Open-Book T-Spine Rotation
    Thoracic Rotation · Shoulder on the Stretch
    8 each side

    Side-lying, knees stacked and bent, arms straight out in front. Open the top arm across to the other side and follow it with your eyes. Let the ribcage turn, keep the knees down.

    The stretch at the bag is a rotation as much as a split — and a stiff mid-back makes the shoulder pay for it.
  • 16b · Prone Y-T-W
    Mid-back and rear shoulder STRENGTH
    2 × 8 each letter

    Face down on the floor or a bench. Thumbs up, lift the arms into a Y, then a T, then a W. Small range, no momentum, pause at the top. Two-pound plates are plenty. Ego has no role here.

    Holds the new thoracic range in place, and doubles as posterior-cuff work on a lift day.
  • 17a · Wrist Flexor + Extensor Stretch
    Forearm · Wrist
    30s each direction

    Arm straight out, palm up, gently pull the fingers back and down. Then flip the palm down and pull the other way. Both directions, both arms.

    First base takes more picks and short hops than any other spot on the field. The wrist absorbs every one of them.
  • 17b · Reverse Curl + Wrist Roller
    Forearm and grip STRENGTH
    2 × 12

    Reverse curl with a light bar or dumbbells, palms down. Follow with a wrist roller up and down once each way if you have one — a bar with string and a plate does the job.

    Closes the loop with J-Band #12. A forearm you have strengthened absorbs a bad hop instead of transmitting it.

J-Bands · Official 11 + Optional Extras

Created by Alan Jaeger. Bauer's been doing this exact routine since age 12. The bands have light tension by design — these target small rotator cuff muscles, not biceps.

🧢 Kid Rules — same moves, your size
For you
  • Lightest band only. If it burns or your arm shakes, it's too strong — switch down.
  • 10–15 easy reps, not 25. Smooth beats strong. Slow like syrup.
  • Do it with a grown-up and count out loud together.
  • This is a warmup, never a workout — you should finish feeling ready, not tired.
  • Try to beat yesterday's smoothest rep, not yesterday's hardest pull.
Your checkmarks count just like the pros' — check them off and watch the shelf. ⭐
Protocol
Standard
  • Reps: 25 per exercise (1 set)
  • Tension: light — band at ~2× resting length, not stretched to failure
  • When: before every throwing session; daily during season is fine
  • Pace: slow and controlled — 2 sec concentric, 2 sec eccentric
  • Setup: clip carabiner to fence/pole at chest height, velcro both wrists
If 25 reps feels too easy, ADD reps (35, 45). Don't add band tension — bigger muscles will take over and defeat the purpose.

  • 01 · Over-the-head Forearm Extensions
    Triceps · Lat · Posterior Shoulder
    25 reps

    Face away from anchor, bands behind you. Arms up overhead, elbows bent. Extend forearms forward over the head, then return slowly.

    Why: opens up the back of the shoulder and warms triceps before any throwing motion
  • 02 · Side Extensions (HELD)
    Lat Stretch · Posterior Capsule
    2 × 30s

    Stand sideways to anchor. Far arm overhead, lean opposite way. HELD STRETCH — breathe into it. 2 × 30 seconds each side.

    Not reps. This is a stretch, not a contraction.
  • 03 · Diagonal Extensions (HELD)
    Lat · T-Spine
    2 × 30s

    Similar to side extension but pull on a diagonal across the body. HELD STRETCH — 2 × 30 seconds each side. Breathe through it.

    Opens thoracic spine rotation — the missing link in most adult throwers.
  • 04 · Forward Flies
    Anterior Deltoid · Pec
    25 reps

    Face away from anchor, arms out to the sides at shoulder height. Bring the palms together in front of you, then slow return.

    Why: chest + front of the shoulder — the pressing / finishing side of the throw
  • 05 · Reverse Flies
    Posterior Deltoid · Rhomboids
    25 reps

    Face the anchor, arms forward at shoulder height. Pull straight back and apart, squeezing the shoulder blades. Slow return.

    Why: rear delts + upper back — balances all the front-side throwing and pressing
  • 06 · Internal Rotation
    Subscapularis · Anterior Cuff
    25 reps

    Stand sideways, throwing arm closer to anchor. Elbow at 90° tucked to side. Rotate forearm IN across the belly.

    Critical for deceleration health — what stops the arm after release
  • 07 · External Rotation
    Infraspinatus · Teres Minor
    25 reps

    Same setup but anchor on opposite side. Elbow tucked to side at 90°. Rotate forearm OUT away from belly.

    The MVP exercise for thrower health. If only one J-Band could be done, this is it.
  • 08 · Elevated Internal Rotation
    Subscapularis @ 90° abduction
    25 reps

    Arm raised to shoulder height (90° abducted), elbow bent 90°. Forearm starts up, rotates DOWN/forward.

    Mimics late cocking position — the highest-strain phase of the throw
  • 09 · Elevated Internal Rotation (Alt)
    Subscapularis · Different Angle
    25 reps

    Same position, alternate motion path — emphasizes a slightly different fiber angle of subscap. Bauer keeps both.

    Redundancy is the point — small muscles benefit from varied angles
  • 10 · Elevated External Rotation
    Infraspinatus @ 90° abduction
    25 reps

    Arm at 90° abduction, elbow bent. Forearm starts down/forward, rotates UP and back.

    This is the "lay-back" position — protects against UCL stress
  • 11 · Reverse + Forward Throwing
    Decel Chain · Full Pattern
    25 + 25

    Part A — Reverse throwing: Face the anchor. Mimic throwing motion in reverse, slowly — start at finish position, work back through release, layback, cocking.

    Part B — Forward throwing: Face away from anchor. Mimic full throwing motion against band resistance. Slow and controlled — loading the pattern.

    The exit ramp — band-loaded full motion before live throws
  • 12 · Wrist / Forearm Series (BONUS)
    Forearm + Wrist Stabilizers
    25 each

    Pronation, supination, wrist flexion, wrist extension. Light resistance, full range. 25 each direction.

    Not in original Jaeger 11, but critical for release point control. Especially important for 1B (pick scoops) and catchers (snap throws).
  • Optional extras · woven in below · counted only when ON
  • Rows (EXTRA)
    Rhomboids · Mid-Back
    25 reps

    Band anchored in front at chest height. Pull the elbows straight back, squeeze the shoulder blades, slow return.

  • Face Pulls (EXTRA)
    Rear Delt · Rotator Cuff
    25 reps

    Anchor at face height. Pull the band toward your face, elbows high, rotating the hands back at the end. Slow return.

  • Shoulder Circles (EXTRA)
    Cuff Activation
    10 ea way

    Light band, arms out to the sides. Small, controlled circles — forward, then back. Activation, not strength.

  • Forward and Back (EXTRA)
    Scapular Control
    15 reps

    Arms extended at shoulder height. Glide the shoulder blades forward (protract), then pull them back (retract). Controlled.

  • Triceps Extension (EXTRA)
    Triceps
    25 reps

    Anchor high. Keep the elbow fixed at your side and extend the forearm down against the band. Slow return.

  • A's (EXTRA)
    Lower Trap
    15 reps

    Hinge slightly forward, arms down. Raise the arms back and down into an A shape, thumbs out, squeezing low on the shoulder blades.

  • T's (EXTRA)
    Mid Trap
    15 reps

    Arms straight out to the sides into a T, thumbs up. Squeeze the shoulder blades together, slow return.

  • Y's (EXTRA)

Shoulder Tube

The OG warmup tool Bauer's been using since 2012. Plastic tube with weighted internal ball. Oscillation activates the rotator cuff without any band tension.

Protocol
Standard
  • 4 planes: forward, lateral, overhead, throwing-position
  • Duration: 10 seconds each plane = ~40s total
  • Speed: shake fast — frequency activates the cuff, slow is wasted
  • When: immediately after dynamic warmup, before J-Bands
No shoulder tube? ~$60 at Driveline. Substitute = vigorous arm circles + plyo ball figure-8s overhead.
  • 01 · Forward Plane
    Anterior Cuff
    10 sec

    Arm straight out in front, shoulder height. Tube parallel to ground. Shake forward-back fast.

    💬 Kid cue: shake it like you're flicking water off your hand — fast little buzzes, not big waves.
  • 02 · Lateral Plane
    Lateral (Middle) Deltoid · Supraspinatus
    10 sec

    Arm out to the side, shoulder height. Tube parallel. Shake side-to-side fast.

    💬 Kid cue: arm out like an airplane wing — buzz the wing as fast as you can.
  • 03 · Overhead
    Full Cuff @ overhead position
    10 sec

    Arm straight up overhead. Tube horizontal across the top. Shake forward-back.

    💬 Kid cue: hold it up like a superhero antenna and make it hum.
  • 04 · Throwing Position
    Lay-back position cuff
    10 sec

    Arm in "L" — elbow at shoulder height, forearm vertical (cocking position). Shake.

    This plane is sport-specific — it's the only one not in general rehab protocols
    💬 Kid cue: make your throwing "L" and shake till it tingles — that's your arm waking up.
★ PITCHER ADD

Bauer adds a 5th plane: finish position — arm extended forward at hip height, shake hard for 10 sec. Mimics decel chain. Only for pitchers / max-effort throwers.

PlyoCare

Trevor Bauer's daily weighted-ball routine, adapted from his Baseball 401 · Daily Weighted Ball Routine video. The logic is back-chaining — start at the very last action of the delivery (the arm/glove finish) and work backward, drill by drill, until you've rebuilt the whole delivery. He runs it every day to groove sequencing before catch play.

Youth: light catch / play only
Under ~13

Heavy plyo balls are not for kids. Skip the weighted-ball routine entirely — light catch and free play build a healthy young arm better than load work. The full prescriptions below unlock on an adult profile.

The 6-Ball Set
From the video
  • 2000 g · 71 oz — Bauer's "4 lb" — kneeling reverse + spiral warm-up
  • 1000 g · 35 oz — Bauer's "2 lb" — sequence drills (standing / stomp / hop-step)
  • 450 g · 16 oz — Bauer's "1 lb" — drop-step pickoffs
  • 225 g · 8 oz — drop drill
  • 150 g · 5 oz — step-behind w/ arm swing
  • 100 g · 3.5 oz — mound / stretch throws
Weights ramp the routine from heavy & slow (rotational warm-up) down to light & fast (full-delivery throws) — heavier first to groove the pattern, lighter last to add speed.

The Routine (in order)

  • 01 · Kneeling Reverse Throw
    Core / Rotational Warm-up
    10R+10L · 2000g/4lb · RPE 2

    Front knee locked to the ground for a stable base. Open the T-spine and throw across your body. Bauer uses this as a core / rotational warm-up — it is NOT a shoulder drill.

    Stable base · open the T-spine · rotate, don't reach
  • 02 · Spiral Drill
    Elbow Spiral · Rotation Point
    10R+10L · 2000g/4lb · RPE 3

    Yank the glove side first to end range — that's your rotation point. Lead with the back of the elbow (the elbow spiral) and throw STRAIGHT into the wall so it bounces straight back. Do NOT catch off the wall — catch behind your back.

    Glove side to end range · lead with the elbow · catch behind the back
  • 03 · Reverse + Spiral, repeat lighter
    Ramp Down a Weight
    10 rev + 10 spiral / side · 1000g/2lb · RPE 4

    Same two drills, same cues — drop to the lighter ball and run both again, each side. This is the ramp: heavy to groove it, lighter to add speed.

    Same cues as 01 & 02 · lighter ball · keep it crisp
  • 04 · Standing Sequence
    Shoulder-Hip Separation
    5 · 1000g/2lb · RPE 4

    MOVE THE HIPS FIRST. Do not let the torso or arms move until the hips move. Turn the front foot in to start the swivel. This trains shoulder-hip separation and the sequence of the delivery.

    Hips first · front foot turns in · torso and arm wait
  • 05 · Stomp Sequence
    Stretch-Shortening Cycle
    5 · 1000g/2lb · RPE 4-5

    Attack the ground with the front foot — stomp — to drive hip rotation. Back foot points at the target, hips at roughly 45°. The stomp loads the stretch-shortening cycle.

    Stomp the front foot · back foot at target · hips ~45°
  • 06 · Hop-Step (Javelin Step) Sequence
    Block → Hip Rotation
    5 · 1000g/2lb · RPE 4-5

    Keep the back foot pointed at the target on landing and land with hips at ~45° — do NOT square off. Blocking the front leg is what drives the hip rotation.

    Back foot at target on landing · hips ~45° · block the front leg
  • 07 · Drop-Step Pickoffs
    Hips Start the Delivery
    5 · 450g/1lb · RPE 5-6

    Simulates the hips starting the delivery into the block. Drop the back foot behind and gain ground toward the target. This one starts closed (the sequence drills start open). Order stays the same: hips → torso → shoulder → arm.

    Start closed · drop back foot, gain ground · hips → torso → shoulder → arm
  • 08 · Drop Drill
    The Drop Phase · Absorb
    5 · 225g/8oz · RPE 5-6

    Single-leg depth drop off a box (no box: pick the back foot up, lift the front foot, land on the back foot). This is the drop phase — absorb the energy. Do NOT push or triple-extend; let the back leg rotate the back hip. It's rotational even though it looks linear.

    Absorb, don't push · no triple extension · back leg rotates the back hip
  • 09 · Step-Behind w/ Arm Swing
    Add Energy → Block It
    5 · 150g/5oz · RPE 5-6

    Now add energy and block it. Step behind, land and accept the energy in the back leg, sink as the back hip rotates, then the front foot contacts and drives the hips into the upper half.

    Accept energy in the back leg · sink as the hip rotates · front foot drives the upper half
  • 10 · Mound / Stretch Throws
    Full Delivery · Integrate
    5 · 100g/3.5oz · RPE 6

    Take it to the mound (or flat ground) and throw out of the stretch: lift leg → drop phase → rotation → block the front leg. This integrates everything — the back-chain is complete and you've rebuilt the full delivery.

    Out of the stretch · lift → drop → rotate → block · everything together
  • Bonus · Humpty Drill
    Lunge/Receive · Indoor Tunnel
    8-12 reps

    Indoor tunnel plyoball drill: lunge/receive position with a pink 0.5 LB ball. Includes ball selection explanation (all hand sizes) and outdoor wall throws. From Bauer's "Rogue Umpire" vlog, Day 2 pregame.

    A supplemental plyoball drill outside the standard 10-step routine. Not a replacement for any step above -- pure bonus.

Position-Specific Emphasis

★ PITCHER — FULL BACK-CHAIN

Run all 10 drills in order, Kneeling Reverse Throw through Mound / Stretch Throws. This IS Bauer's delivery rebuild — heavy and slow to groove the pattern, lighter and faster down to a full throw off the mound. The program as designed.

★ CATCHER — SEQUENCE FOCUS

Throw is shorter and quicker than a pitcher's. Lean on the warm-up and sequence work (Kneeling Reverse Throw, Spiral, the Standing / Stomp / Hop-Step sequences) and the lighter balls — that quick transfer is the priority over the full mound throw.

★ 1B — LIGHT / RECOVERY

Shortest throws on the field. Use the rotational warm-up (Kneeling Reverse Throw, Spiral) and the lighter balls, fewer reps. Plyo here is recovery and decel work, not arm-strength building — no need to chase the full back-chain down to the mound.

★ 2B — SHORT / SHARP

The DP turn is a short, sharp, off-balance throw. Get value from the sequence drills and Drop-Step Pickoffs (which start closed, like a turn) on the lighter balls and fewer reps. The deep-hole power of the full mound throw isn't your job.

★ SS — FULL BACK-CHAIN

Longest infield throws and the most variable angles — deep in the hole, on the run. Treat the arm like a pitcher's: run all 10 drills in order through Mound / Stretch Throws.

★ 3B — NEAR-FULL

The cross-diamond throw is closer to a pitch than a flip. Run the sequence and drop-step work and carry it down through the lighter, faster drills to a Mound / Stretch Throw. Effectively the full back-chain.

★ OF — FULL BACK-CHAIN

OF throws are the hardest in the game — full effort, full crow-hop, max distance. Run all 10 drills in order through Mound / Stretch Throws. The complete heavy-to-light sequence is exactly what protects the arm.

Don't use plyo balls cold. The J-Band routine MUST come first. Plyo balls will hurt an unwarmed shoulder fast.

Long Toss

Bauer's long-toss program is based on Jaeger's Year-Round Throwing program. The principle: build distance gradually, then "pull down" with hard throws at a shorter distance.

🛑 Kid Throwing Rules — the ones that keep your arm
Always
  • Pain = stop. Never throw through arm pain — you're done for the day, and you don't throw again until it's gone completely.
  • Tired = done. A tired arm is the signal to quit, not push.
  • Rest after big days. A day or two off after a lot of throwing — backyard throws count too.
  • Every throw counts. Practice, warmups, backyard — not just game pitches. Tell your parent/coach how much you threw.
  • No pitching 3 days in a row, and only one game a day.
📈 How kids actually get better
In order
  • 1. Control — throw it where you want, easy effort.
  • 2. Command — hit the exact glove spot, every time.
  • 3. Speed — only after 1 and 2. Velocity comes last, not first.
  • Catch play IS training at 8–12: play a lot, have fun, play other sports too — that builds the athlete the arm hangs on.
  • Fastball + changeup only until 14 — no curveballs (that's the doctors AND the pros agreeing).
  • Stretch it out gently: when you long toss, add distance a little at a time at easy effort — and the moment it hurts or your arm's tired, the STOP rules above win. (Grown-up throwers argue about how far kids should stretch it; nobody argues about stopping at pain.)
🗓 The season rules
Every year
  • 4+ months per year completely OFF from overhead throwing — at least 2–3 of them back-to-back. Best arm-saver there is.
  • One team at a time — doubling up leagues is how young arms get cooked.
  • Don't catch (the position) on days you also pitch.
  • Innings ceilings for the year: about 60 (age 8 & under) · 80 (9–12) · 100 (13–14).

The Ladder
Standard

Start at 60 ft. Add 15 ft per round. 5 throws per distance.

  • 60 ft · 5 throws · mechanics check
  • 75 ft · 5 throws · build rhythm
  • 90 ft · 5 throws · find the arc
  • 105 ft · 5 throws · loose arc, no max effort
  • 120 ft · 5 throws · sustained reach
  • 135 ft+ · 5 throws · extension day only
Then PULL DOWN: 5 throws at 90 ft hitting partner's chest, then 5 at 60 ft on a line. This is where arm strength gets locked in.
★ PITCHER PROGRAM

3 days/week max. Bullpen day + long-toss day + light recovery day. Don't long-toss within 24 hrs of a game start.

★ CATCHER PROGRAM

Long-toss 1-2 days/week, max to 150 ft. Most game throws are short and explosive. Building extension carry helps the throw to 2B feel effortless.

★ 1B PROGRAM

Light long-toss, max to 120 ft. Throws don't require carry. But long-toss still keeps the arm durable through a season.

★ 2B PROGRAM

Max to 135 ft. Build the relay-throw distance, since 2B is a cutoff target for OF throws.

★ SS PROGRAM

Max to 165 ft. Full pitcher-equivalent ladder. The deep-hole-to-1B throw IS a long-toss throw on the run.

★ 3B PROGRAM

Max to 150 ft. Cross-diamond at game speed is 120 ft — warmup ladder must exceed that.

★ OF PROGRAM

The longest-tossers in the game. Max to 200+ ft in warmup. Bauer's full ladder applies. Long-toss days at least 2x/week in-season.

The Routine (in order)

  • 01 · Daily Measurements
    Sleep · HRV · strength baseline
    ~2 min

    Before anything, Bauer logs his numbers: sleep (Oura ring), HRV (morning HR strap), shoulder strength in internal & external rotation (a MicroFET II dynamometer), grip strength (full-hand + two-finger, for forearm readiness) and a counter-movement jump on force plates. A bad number and he adjusts the day's warm-up.

    Measure first · let the data set the day
  • 02 · Hot Tub + Dress
    Raise core temp · optional
    personal

    Bauer sits in a hot tub, then gets into his uniform — undershirt, shorts — before heading to the weight room. He's the first to say most people don't have a hot tub before a game, so don't sweat this one. The point is simply to raise core temperature a little before mobility work.

    Warm the body before you ask it to move
  • 03 · Foam Roll + T-Spine
    Mobility · thoracic rotation
    few min

    Into the weight room (music on — he treats it as part of the build). Foam-roll routine first, then a T-spine drill: rotate the thoracic spine only — don't let the hips move at all. Pure upper-back rotation to free the throwing motion.

    Roll out · then rotate the T-spine, hips quiet
  • 04 · Hip Mobility
    Open the hips
    few min

    Open the hips through as much range as possible, with a little external rotation in the right (drive-leg) hip. On a game day he modifies the range — enough to feel loose without digging into deep end-range before he has to compete.

    Mobilize, don't max out — it's game day
  • 05 · Core Activation · Scorpion
    Mobility + core activation
    Bauer's favorite

    His favorite: swing the leg out to the side and behind into a scorpion-like position, rocking forward and back, then plant the toe behind. The whole time the hips are pressed off the ground by the bottom leg — so the core stays active while the hips and T-spine move through range. Mobility and activation at once.

    Hips off the floor · core works through the range
  • 06 · Cuff Weights
    Rotator-cuff prep · 10 lb
    10 lb · 4 drills

    Last thing before leaving the weight room — a 10 lb cuff-weight series, four drills: (1) swings, up and down, to loosen everything; (2) two-hand overhead throws; (3) single-arm rotational throw for the right shoulder; (4) internal-rotation work. It's enough to break a sweat and get him breathing hard.

    Swings → overhead → single-arm rotation → internal rotation
  • 07 · Weighted Balls
    CORE warm-up · sequencing
    ~10 each · black + green

    Full uniform on, back to the weight room. Key point: by now the shoulders are already warm — he uses the weighted balls as a CORE warm-up, for rotation both directions. Reverse throws right hand then left; then ~10 reverse + 10 pivot-picks with the black ball (both hands), ~10 reverse + 10 pivot-picks with the green. Then a walking sequence drill — block the front leg, turn the hips, that turns the shoulders, that delivers the arm — ramping intensity, finishing with 3–5 deliveries off the green ball.

    Shoulders already warm · this is rotation + sequencing
  • 08 · Shoulder Tube
    On-field · before every game
    full routine

    Back to the locker for cleats, the rest of the uniform, the shoulder tube and headphones (pregame playlist). Out to the field, and in front of the dugout he runs his full shoulder-tube routine — every game. The oscillating tube wakes up the stabilizers right before he throws for real.

    Stabilizers on · in front of the dugout
  • 09 · Long Toss
    Stretch out · pull down
    ~50 throws

    Out to max distance — some days he'll set a long-toss record — then he pulls down, progressing back in with hard, on-a-line effort throws. It works out to about 50 throws total in the long-toss session, in the natural cadence of getting fully warm.

    Stretch out to max · then pull down on a line
  • 10 · Pregame Bullpen
    Sequencing & command
    ~42 throws

    Catch his breath, then the mound. He starts to a corner of the zone and works in. Unlike long toss, this is about sequencing and command — he throws realistic sequences (e.g. fastball up-and-in to a righty, then cutter, then slider), working in every pitch, fewer changeups. ~42 throws. The mantra: the bullpen is for warming up, not for hitting every spot — you don't get anyone out in the pen. Be physically ready to compete in the game.

    Warm up the sequencing · spots come in the game

The Five Principles

01 ; Hands First, Then Feet

Start on knees. No footwork. Glove only. Then graduate to standing without glove. Then standing with glove. Train each layer before adding the next. Most coaches train footwork and hands at the same time; Wash isolates them.

02 ; The 15-Inch Zone

The ideal short hop catches just in front of the knee. Within a 15-inch rectangular zone, you can funnel, transfer, throw. Two inches farther out, you're reaching. Two inches in, you're handcuffed. The zone defines the work.

03 ; "That's On Time / That's Late"

Wash's actual coaching cadence. As soon as the ball touches the ground, the glove must already be moving through the line. Late = bobble or boot. On time = subtle push through the hop (3-6 inches), nothing more. Listen for the sound; if the sound between ball-ground and ball-glove changes, the timing is off.

04 ; Create a Backboard With Your Glove

Glove fingers down. Position the glove DIRECTLY behind the hop. Push through the line with a wrist angle that can block the ball even if you don't squeeze. The glove becomes a wall; the ball can't pass.

05 ; Attack The Ball

Wash's words: "go to the ball; don't let the ball come to you." The word attack sounds violent, but it's not. It's about closing the gap to take a clean hop instead of an in-between hop. Cut the angle. Move.

The Core Progression (Daily, 4-5 min)

Simple
Full method

Same sequence for every position. Tap the box to mark done. Tap the name for details.

  • 00 · The Arrow
    The Idea Every Other Drill Serves
    before you start

    Before the ball is hit, know the line you have to travel to get to it — and then travel it. Straight there. Not out and back in, not around it. Washington opens and closes the whole session on this: draw your arrow where you are supposed to go, then go.

    This is not a drill, it is the reason the drills exist. He returns to it twice in eleven minutes, which is how you know it is the spine and not a tip.
  • 01–03 · Knee Short Hops
    Hands only · on the knees · 8–10 ft apart

    Both partners on knees. Partner throws firm short hops; glove fingers down, push through the line of the hop. No footwork — just hands. Work all three angles from the same position, ticking each as you go.

    One video for all three: the source is a single continuous kneeling block and does not cleanly separate the sides, so labelling three clips would claim a precision the tape does not have.

  • Center
    At your center mass · 10–15 reps
    10-15

    Straight at the chest. Push through the line of the hop.

    The foundation. Every other Washington Drill builds on this.
  • Forehand
    Glove side · 10 reps
    10

    Reach out with the glove only; don't shift your body. Catch in the 15-inch zone, push through the hop.

    Trains reach without losing the catch position.
  • Backhand
    Backhand side · 10 reps
    10

    The hardest of the three; the wrist angle has to be right or the ball squirts past.

    Catch it in the same part of the glove every time — the middle, with the hands — not out in the webbing. The wall principle still applies.
  • 04 ; Stand, No Glove ; Bare Hand
    Hand Path ; 10 reps
    10

    Stand up. Drop the glove. Partner rolls slower short hops. Catch barehand. Force the hand path to be correct, since you can't fake it with a glove.

    The deceptively important step. Glove forgives bad habits; bare hand exposes them.
  • 05 ; Stand, Glove ; Short Hop Mix
    Full Pattern ; 15-20 reps
    15-20

    Glove back on. Standing. Partner throws short hops mixed across center, forehand, backhand. No predictability. React.

    Now you're game-state. All five principles working at once.
  • 06 ; Number-The-Hops Drill
    Eyes + Tracking ; 10 reps
    10

    Wash + Semien drill. Take ground balls (or short hops). Read the bounces and call out the LAST hop — the one you'll actually field — before it reaches you. Wash: the only thing the infielder should worry about is the last hop.

    Trains your eyes to read the bounce and commit to the hop you field.
  • 07 · Force In, Force Out
    Working Both Directions Deliberately
    8-10 each way

    From the kneeling or short standing position, drive in toward the hop, then work back out away from it. Both directions on purpose, not just whichever way the ball happens to go.

    Stops you only ever practising the hop that comes to you.
  • 08 · Pad Work · Downhill and the Skip
    Reading the Hop Before It Arrives
    2-3 min

    Partner throws off a pad so the ball skips. Watch the skip and read where it is going while it is still travelling. Work downhill through it — get on top rather than reaching under.

    The pad is what turns a clean short-hop drill into a real one. Real infields produce bad hops; a flat gym floor does not.
  • 09 · Never From A Standstill
    Move First, Then Field
    every rep from here on

    From this point nothing starts flat-footed. When the ball goes up, you go. Wait for it standing still and you have already given away the step that would have got you there.

    Washington is explicit: he does not want it from a standstill. Every drill above gets harder and more honest once this rule is on.
  • 10 · Glove Side · Go Get It And Keep Going
    Lateral, Through The Ball
    8-10 each side

    Break to the glove side, pick it up, and keep going through it — do not field it and stop. The catch is a point you pass through, not a place you arrive at.

    He calls the finished version painting a picture: one continuous line rather than a set of separate movements stapled together.
  • 12 · Crossover Backhand · Cross And Go
    Backhand Under Real Range
    6-8 reps

    Ball goes up, you take off — crossover step, then the backhand. Not a reach from where you stand; a crossover that covers ground and arrives with the glove already working down the line.

    This is the backhand from step 03 after the arrow, the movement and the range have all been added to it.
  • 11 · Fungo Feeds · Feet Moving
    Live Off The Bat
    3-4 min

    Off the fungo now, not hand-fed. Keep your feet moving right up until you are ready to catch it. Live contact changes the timing enough that hand-fed reps alone will not prepare you.

    The first drill on this page where the ball behaves the way it will in a game.

Position-Specific Adaptations

★ ALL POSITIONS

The core progression above applies to every infield position. The position-specific work below adds on after the foundation. Tap your position at the top to filter the additional drills.

★ 1B ; SCHANUEL / FREEMAN ADD

Pick Series (Wash + Freeman + Schanuel): After the core, run the same short-hop progression but with one player throwing from "across the diamond" distance (~70-90 ft). Catch becomes a pick. Same 15-inch zone, same backboard principle.

Stretch + Mark: Both feet on the bag, mark your max stretch with a glove or chalk. Partner throws to the mark. 20 reps. Wash's principle: heels on corners, stride is glove-side only, throwing-side foot anchors.

★ 2B ; ALBIES ADD

DP Feed Progression (Wash's specialty with Albies and Swanson): Four positions; (1) face right; underhand flip left, (2) face right; field grounder + flip 2B feed, (3) face left; pivot to knee, drop step, lower arm slot for SS-to-2B feed, (4) face left; drop step + open up the body for 2B-to-SS DP feed.

Quick Hands Series: From standing, partner throws short hops at 6 ft (closer than core). Force max-speed transfer. Catch + simulated throw in one motion.

★ SS ; SWANSON / SEMIEN ADD

Backhand + Plant: Wash's signature deep-hole drill. From standing position, partner rolls slow grounders to the backhand side at increasing depth. Field on the run, plant the throwing-side foot, throw across. 8 reps.

DP Pivot Variants: Same as 2B's DP progression but reversed. Wash insists on practicing both sides; SS and 2B share the work.

Number The Hops (Semien's specific add): Every ground ball ; count the hops out loud. Trains reads on long throws.

★ 3B ; RILEY ADD

Short-Hop Gauntlet (Wash with Riley, World Series 2021): Standing, partner throws faster and harder short hops at game-distance reaction time. 20-30 reps in a 60-second burst. Wash's signature pre-game work for the hot corner.

Slow Roller Rake: After short-hop work, charge a slow rolled ball. Two-step into it, sweep the glove through the ball on the glove side (or barehand a dead ball), funnel to the throwing hand, two-step throw on the run.

★ PITCHER NOTE

Pitcher fielding practice (PFP) borrows the Washington short-hop foundation but adds covering 1B and fielding bunts. Run drills 1-3 (knee progression) as part of pre-bullpen work; full PFP is a separate session.

★ CATCHER NOTE

Blocking drills are catcher-specific; not Washington Drills. But the short-hop foundation (drills 1-3) directly transfers to receiving balls in the dirt. Wash's "create a backboard" principle is the same idea catchers use for blocks.

★ OF NOTE

Wash's drills are infield-only by design. Outfielders should still do the knee short-hop progression to keep hands sharp; OF-specific work (drop step, tracking, crow hop) is separate.

Second Base · Washington with Luis Arraez

Same coach, same session format, a different position. Most of what he says here you have already met above -- downhill, go straight to it, the arrow. That repetition is the point: the method does not change by position, the geometry does. What follows is only the part that is genuinely second base, plus the two errors he corrects most.

All optional / +bonus -- this block is for infielders working the middle, not a requirement for everyone.

  • 2B-1 · Get The Ball Out Front
    Where You Meet It · Not Between Your Feet
    10-12 reps

    Meet the ball in front of you. The moment you let it get in between your feet you have no adjustment left and no throw. Washington corrects this more than anything else in the session -- three separate times inside the first thirty seconds.

    The single most-repeated correction on the tape. If you take one thing from this block, take this one.
  • 2B-2 · Fungo · Right At You
    Live Off The Bat, Straight On
    2-3 min

    Off the bat now rather than hand-fed, starting with the ball hit right at you before anything lateral. Then back up a step and repeat from depth.

    Straight-on first is deliberate: it removes the footwork variable so the hands and the angle are the only thing being tested.
  • 2B-3 · Go Downhill And Play With It
    Attack The Hop, Do Not Wait For It
    8-10 reps

    Work down through the ball rather than staying level and letting it come to you. He is explicit that he does not want you simply arriving and waiting -- get downhill on it and play with it.

    The same idea as the pad work in the main progression. It is the method in two words: downhill, always.
  • 2B-4 · Go Straight, Not Around
    The Arrow, At Second Base
    8-10 reps

    His correction verbatim: you went this way and then came down -- go straight. Travelling out and then back in costs exactly the time you needed, and the ball does not wait while you take the long route.

    This is step 00 The Arrow applied to the middle infield. Same line, different starting point.
  • 2B-5 · Tall And Narrow Is Trouble
    The Base You Field From
    check every rep

    Two faults he names in the same breath: standing a little too tall, and letting the feet get narrow. Either alone is survivable. Together there is no base to field from and nothing to throw from.

    Worth checking on video. It is the fault you cannot feel while you are making it.
  • 2B-6 · Alignment
    Body, Glove And Target On One Line
    2 min

    He demonstrates it both ways on purpose -- this is alignment, this is not alignment -- because the difference is small enough to stay invisible until you see the wrong version next to the right one.

    He closes the session on this. The last thing a coach says is usually the thing he most wants remembered.

The Wider Giants Infield Program

Attribution first: this is not Ron Washington. These drills come from other Giants infield sessions with different coaches — the 2021 infield program, Volume 2, and individual work with David Villar and LaMonte Wade Jr. Same organisation, same system, different voices. They are kept here so the infield work lives in one place, not because Washington taught them.

Included because each adds something his sessions genuinely do not: a receiving standard (the kill spot), an explicit read rule, constraint games, and the only coaching anywhere in these sources aimed at the person feeding rather than fielding. All optional / +bonus.

  • Hands · everything is preparation for the kill spot
  • GI-1 · One-Handed, In The Middle
    Hands Before Anything Else
    10-12 reps

    Glove hand only, ball straight at the middle. No throwing, no footwork, nothing to hide behind. Then offset yourself to the glove side and do the forehand version of the same thing.

    Every session in this program opens here. Take the feet and the throw away and the hands have nowhere to hide.
  • GI-2 · Bring It To The Center
    The Glove Path · Not Sent Out Late
    10-12 reps

    Work the glove to the centre of your body rather than out and away. The fault being corrected is sending the glove out late — reaching at the ball instead of receiving it in front of your own mass.

    Late glove is the most common receiving fault in the whole program, and the hardest to feel yourself doing.
  • GI-3 · Show The Pocket · Fingers To The Sky
    Glove Angle At The Final Hop
    12-15 reps

    Out in front of the bat, showing the pocket to the ball. At the final hop your fingers should still be to the sky — the moment they turn down early you have closed the glove on a ball that has not arrived.

    The most technical cue in the program, and the one the coach keeps returning to. Fingers down early is a miss you can predict.
  • GI-4 · Stiff Hands Miss
    Relax, Then Find The Range
    8-10 reps

    Relax first, then find the range out in front. Stiff hands cannot adjust to a hop that changes late, and almost every hop changes late.

    Pairs with the step above: the right glove angle held with tension is still the wrong answer.
  • GI-5 · The Kill Spot
    The Same Receiving Position, Every Time

SECTION 02 · FLEXIBILITY

Stretching Protocols

Two flexibility blocks: dynamic warmup (before throwing, movement-based) and static stretching (after throwing, hold-based). Dynamic before games, static after — backed by meta-analysis showing static stretching pre-activity decreases power output 2–5%.

Dynamic Warmup (PRE-Activity)
Before Throwing

5–8 minutes. Goal: blood flow, neuromuscular activation, joint mobility. NO held stretches here.

Static Stretching (POST-Activity)
After Throwing

5–8 minutes. Hold 20–30 seconds per stretch. Goal: lengthen tissue, restore ROM, reduce DOMS. Saves to recovery sessions or post-game.

Pitchers develop reduced external rotation and increased internal rotation in the throwing shoulder, plus limited thoracic mobility. Static stretching directly addresses these imbalances over time.
★ PITCHER PRIORITY

Don't skip the Sleeper Stretch (#07). It's the most-cited shoulder stretch for pitcher longevity. Also prioritize lead-leg Couch Stretch (#08).

★ CATCHER PRIORITY

Calf/dorsiflexion (#12), 90/90 hip (#09), couch stretch (#08). Catchers live in the squat — these are non-negotiable.

★ 3B PRIORITY

Hip mobility (#09) and T-spine rotation (#06 dynamic) — both critical for charging slow rollers and the cross-diamond throw.

Pre-activity vs post-activity: Don't do static stretches before throwing — they reduce explosive power 2–5% per meta-analysis. Save Sleeper Stretch and Couch Stretch for AFTER throwing, recovery sessions, or off-days.

    3 × 12-15

    3 × 15-20

    Biceps Short Head

    Elbow supported and slightly in FRONT of the torso. Full extension at the bottom -- do not cut the rep short to keep the weight moving.

    3 × 12-15

    Palms down, light, strict. Pairs with the wrist work on the Warm Up page.

    Triceps Long Head
    3 × 12-15

    Cable overhead, elbows pointing forward and held still. Deep stretch at the bottom. The long head only gets loaded with the arm overhead.

    A pushdown does not train this head in a stretched position. This one does.

    3 × 10-12

    Shoulders on a bench, dumbbell across the hips on a pad or towel. Squeeze hard at the top and hold a beat, ribs down.

  • Seated Calf Raise
    Soleus
    3 × 15-20

    Seated with the knee bent, which is what shifts the work from the gastroc to the soleus. Machine, or a dumbbell on the knee.

    The standing raise misses the soleus almost entirely. Bent knee is the whole trick.
  • Dead Bug
    Anti-Extension · Rib Position
    3 × 8 each side

    On the back, low back pressed flat and kept there. Extend the opposite arm and leg slowly. The moment the back arches, the set is over.

    Teaches the rib position everything else on this page depends on.

    Anti-Rotation
    3 × 10 each side

    Cable or band at chest height from the side. Press straight out and resist the pull toward the anchor. Do not let the ribs turn.

    Rotation power is built by RESISTING rotation first. This is the drill that transfers to a throw.
