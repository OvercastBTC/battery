The System

Fuel runs on two rails: nitrogen (protein) and water. Both scale with your size (136kg / 300lb), your day's demand (rest vs heavy training), and your environment (Central Valley heat, stimulant load). Miss either and the rest of the stack — peptides, sleep, lifting, coaching Kole in 100°F afternoons — works at a discount.

Daily baseline · 136kg adult
Confirmed
  • Protein: 150–180g muscle preservation · 180–220g recomp/build · spread across 3–5 feedings
  • Water: ~150oz baseline (1oz per 2lb bodyweight) · scales up with heat, training, stimulant load
  • Electrolytes: sodium is the limiting factor in CV heat · 1–4 Liquid IV / LMNT depending on day (LMNT = electrolyte powder packet — 1000mg sodium, no sugar; see Products tab)
  • Cadence: protein in 30–40g pulses every 3–4 hours beats one big load at dinner
  • Markers that matter more than numbers: pale-yellow urine, no headache, no afternoon crash, no cramps
"You can't out-supplement an empty tank. Peptides repair tissue out of substrate — adequate protein is the substrate."

The Two Big Constraints

★ Stimulant load (Dynavel XR)

Amphetamines suppress thirst cues and increase fluid loss. You can't trust "I'm thirsty" as the trigger — by the time it fires, you're already behind. Time-based intake beats thirst-driven intake: one shaker by mid-morning, one by lunch, one by mid-afternoon, etc.

★ Central Valley heat

Outdoor coaching, baseball practice with Kole, and field work in 95–110°F adds 16–32oz per hour on top of baseline. The electrolyte loss is sodium-dominant; plain water alone can dilute you into a headache. This is what Liquid IV / LMNT are for.

★ Compound days (Travel + Train, Heavy + Travel, etc.)

Days aren't always one thing. A Marriott gym session on a travel day is both Travel AND Train. Tap multiple day-type chips and the tracker takes the highest demand in each category — protein from the higher one, water from the higher one, electrolyte ceiling from the higher one. You don't double-count, you just refuse to leave demand on the table.

Why this matters now

Tissue repair needs substrate. The BPC-157 / TB-500 protocol is asking your body to lay down new collagen and remodel labrum tissue. That work happens at the amino-acid level. Running 120g/day when the target is 180g leaves the peptides bidding on materials that aren't there. The most underrated "peptide hack" is hitting protein consistently.

Timing — the real lever

Pulses, not dumps
MPS

Muscle protein synthesis (MPS) responds to a leucine threshold — roughly 2.5–3g leucine per feeding, which is ~30–40g of high-quality protein. Stacking 80g into one shake doesn't double MPS; the surplus oxidizes to fuel or excretes as urea.

  • 3–5 feedings/day, 30–40g each = the practical pattern
  • Don't skip breakfast and back-load — backloading the day leaves muscle in a low-protein state for 12+ hours
  • One scoop Thorne = 21g and ~2.3g leucine — just under the threshold; pair with food
  • Two scoops Thorne = 42g and ~4.6g leucine — solidly above
Hitting 180g across 5 feedings of 36g each > hitting 220g in two big meals.

"Can I do 4 scoops in one 22oz shaker?"

Short answer: physically possible, practically bad idea. The math and the mouthfeel both push back.

Why 4 scoops in one bottle doesn't work
Skip
  • Volume: Thorne's own instructions say "≥8oz liquid per scoop." 4 scoops needs ≥32oz; your shaker is 22oz.
  • Clumping: 112g of powder into 18oz of water = paste, not shake. The mixing ball can't break it up.
  • GI distress: 84g+ of whey isolate in one sitting is a known osmotic-load issue — cramping, bloat, sometimes urgent bathroom trips.
  • Wasted MPS: Past ~40g in one dose, the extra mostly oxidizes. You're paying for protein your muscles don't use.
  • Mixing the deck wrong: The whole point is pulses spaced through the day.

What to do instead

Run a 2-scoop max per shaker, twice a day, plus food. Two 42g shakes (morning + post-practice or mid-afternoon) + two protein-forward meals (~30–40g each) lands you at 150–170g without forcing anything. Add one more scoop or another high-protein snack to push toward 180–200g.

The protein-shake question

Yes, shake water counts. Your body doesn't care if H₂O came from a glass, a shaker bottle, coffee, or watermelon. Three 22oz shakes = 66oz already in the tank — 33–44% of a normal day's target. The old "protein dehydrates you" line is overblown; the kidney load is already baked into normal recommendations at this intake.

Electrolytes — the actually-limiting factor

Liquid IV math
500mg Na / stick

Per stick: 500mg sodium, 370mg potassium, 11g sugar, ~45 cal.

  • Normal day: 1–2 sticks (morning + post-workout or mid-afternoon)
  • Hot day with Kole / outdoor coaching: 2–3 sticks
  • All-day heat / extended field work: 3–4 sticks max
Sodium ceiling: 3 sticks = 1,500mg, leaving room for food. 4 sticks = 2,000mg — fine on a sweat-heavy day, tight if you're also eating salty restaurant food. Sugar load is the silent limiter at 11g × 3 = 33g.
LMNT alternative
1000mg Na / stick

Per stick: 1,000mg sodium, 200mg potassium, 60mg magnesium, 0g sugar, 10 cal.

Double the sodium per stick, no sugar. One LMNT + one Liquid IV often beats three Liquid IVs — same total electrolytes, half the sugar.

Hyponatremia is the real risk at your volume. 200+ oz of plain water at 300lb, on a stimulant, in CV heat is enough to dilute serum sodium meaningfully. Symptoms hit as headache, fatigue, brain fog, and cramps long before anything dramatic — but those are the same symptoms most people blame on dehydration and "solve" by drinking more plain water, making it worse.

Markers that beat the numbers

  • Urine: pale straw yellow. Clear = over-diluted. Dark amber = behind.
  • Cadence: peeing every 2–3 hours during the day.
  • No headache, no 3pm crash, no calf cramps = signal you're in the zone.
  • Bodyweight: if you drop 2+ lbs across a hot afternoon, that's mostly water — replace 1.5× the weight lost.

How do they get 42g into a 14oz bottle?

Ultra-filtration. Real milk is run through soft membrane filters that hold back the large protein molecules (casein and whey) and let through the smaller molecules (water, lactose sugar, some minerals). Run it long enough and you're left with milk that's been protein-concentrated 3–4× from the natural starting point. There are no added protein powders or isolates — it's just fewer of everything else.

The lactose is removed two ways: a chunk is filtered out with the water and sugars, and a lactase enzyme is added to break down what's left. That's why it's labeled lactose-free even though it's literally concentrated milk.

The shelf-stable trick is separate: aseptic packaging + ultra-high-temperature (UHT) pasteurization. Sterile product into a sterile bottle in a sterile environment = no refrigeration needed until opened.

Storage

Unopened: 9 months at room temperature for the 42g Elite (12 months for the 26g standard). Cool, dry, out of direct sun. Keep them in the truck if you want — temperature swings are fine, freezing is not.

Opened: refrigerate and consume within 14 days, same as any milk. Once the aseptic seal breaks, it's just milk again.

Heat Safety

Exertional heat illness is what this app's numbers quietly add up to — but never say out loud. Recognition + prevention, tuned to who's playing. Informational only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.

Heat illness is a Swiss-cheese failure — several layers lining up at once. The app already knows yours:

Recognize it — before it's an emergency

Heat exhaustion
treat on site

Signs: heavy sweating; cool, pale, clammy skin; fast weak pulse; nausea; muscle cramps; headache; dizziness; feeling wiped out.

Do: STOP. Get to shade / AC. Loosen clothing. Cool the skin — wet cloths, cool water, fan. Sip water with some salt / electrolyte. Most people turn around in ~30 min. If it gets worse, you vomit, or it lasts past an hour → get medical help.
⚠ Heat stroke — 911 emergency

The line is the brain. Heat exhaustion + confusion, slurred speech, stumbling, acting "off," or passing out = heat stroke. Skin is hot (in hard exertion it may still be sweaty). Core temp around 104°F and climbing. It kills or disables fast.

① CALL 911 NOW. ② COOL IMMEDIATELY — cold water / ice over the whole body; do not wait for the ambulance. The pros call it "cool first, transport second": dropping core temp in the first ~30 minutes (the "golden half-hour") is what saves the brain. Cold-water immersion cools fastest.

Prevent it today

  • Acclimatize. Build heat exposure over 1–2 weeks — the first hot days, and the first hot day after a cool stretch, are the most dangerous.
  • Drink to thirst — don't force it. Chugging plain water past thirst can drop your blood sodium dangerously (hyponatremia). "You can't drink too much" is wrong.
  • Replace salt, not just water. Heavy sweat = sodium out. That's what your LMNT / Liquid IV logging is for — lean on it on hot, high-sweat days.
  • Work : rest. In real heat, roughly 15–20 min on / 5–10 min in shade with fluid — and more rest as it climbs. Don't grind straight through.
  • Cool actively. Shade, airflow, an ice towel on the neck, a cold drink. You pack the cooler for the kids — use it on yourself too.

Staying safe in the heat

Kids heat up faster than grown-ups, so this is for you! 🌞

Tell a grown-up RIGHT AWAY if you feel dizzy, sick to your stomach, a bad headache, super tired, or fuzzy / confused — or if you stop sweating. Don't tough it out. Getting too hot is serious, and grown-ups can cool you down fast.

Beat the heat
  • 💧 Drink water on a schedule — before you feel thirsty. (Thirsty means you're already behind!)
  • 🌳 Take shade + water breaks, even when you feel fine.
  • 🧢 Take your helmet and heavy gear off during breaks to let the heat out.
  • 🗣️ On really hot days, check in with a grown-up — they're looking out for you.
Informational only — not medical advice. BATTERY is not a medical device; this is recognition + general heat-safety information. In any emergency, call 911. Decisions about your medications (TRT, stimulants, GLP-1) and competing in heat belong with your physician.
